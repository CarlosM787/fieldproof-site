    How the calculator gets from walls to a price

    Five steps, all on your numbers. Nothing here is a typical price or a typical coverage: the paint price and the coverage come from you and your paint can.

    Wall area width × height × how many, for each wall
    Paintable area wall area − (door and window width × height × how many)
    Area to cover paintable area × coats
    Gallons needed area to cover ÷ coverage per gallon × (1 + extra %)
    Gallons to buy gallons needed, rounded up to a whole gallon
    Paint cost gallons to buy × your price per gallon
    Selling price (paint + materials + labor) × (1 + markup) or ÷ (1 − margin)

    Prices from a target margin are rounded up to the next cent, so you never land under the margin you asked for.

    Getting a number you can stand behind

    1. Measure, don’t guess. Width × height for every wall you’ll paint. A room with two pairs of matching walls is two lines: “12 × 8, how many 2” and “11 × 8, how many 2”.
    2. Take out what you won’t paint. Doors, windows, a fireplace face, a built-in. Each is its own line, with a count.
    3. Use your paint’s own coverage. Copy it from the label or the product data sheet. If you’ve painted that kind of wall with that paint before, your own records beat any label: use them, and set the extra % from what you’ve seen.
    4. Price the paint at what you pay. The calculator charges for the whole gallons you buy, not the fraction you use, because that’s what leaves your wallet.
    5. Count the labor, even if it’s you. Prep, cutting in, rolling, cleanup. Your own hours are a cost.

    Questions painters ask

    What coverage per gallon should I use?

    The one on your paint. Coverage changes from product to product, so this calculator doesn’t pre-fill a number. Copy the square feet per gallon from the label or the product data sheet of the paint you’ll actually use.

    Why does it round up to whole gallons?

    Because you buy whole gallons. If the job needs 2.3 gallons, you pay for 3. The calculator shows the exact amount and what’s left over, and prices the paint you buy.

    Does it do ceilings and trim?

    Ceilings, yes: use “I have the total” and enter the ceiling area (length × width), or add it as a “wall” line. Trim is easier to price as labor and materials, since it’s measured by the foot rather than the square foot.

    Should I add primer?

    If the job needs a separate primer, run it as its own estimate with the primer’s coverage and price, or add what you’ll spend on it to “Other materials”.

    Where do the numbers go?

    Nowhere. The math runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent to us. On talkestimate.com we count that the calculator was used, never what you typed.
