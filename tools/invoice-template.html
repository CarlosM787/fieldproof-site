    How the totals add up

    Every amount is one you type. The template only multiplies, adds and subtracts, in a fixed order, so you and your customer can check each line on the printout.

    Line total quantity × unit price (no quantity: the price you typed, a flat price)
    Subtotal every line total, added up
    Sales tax subtotal × tax %, rounded to the cent once
    Total subtotal + sales tax
    Balance due total − payments received

    Each line is rounded to the cent once, and the tax once. A line with no price adds nothing.

    What goes on an invoice, and why

    • Who and which job. Your business, the customer and the job address, plus an invoice number and date, so you can both tell which bill this is.
    • What it bills. The estimate number and any change order numbers, so the customer can match each line to what they approved.
    • One line per item. The work from the estimate and each approved change order, so nothing on the bill is a surprise.
    • What’s already paid. The deposit and any payment since, taken off the total.
    • The balance due, and how to pay it. One clear number, a due date if you use one, and how the customer can pay you.

    The example, checked

    The example is the invoice for the bathroom ceiling job from the free estimate template and the free change order template. Every price is an example, not a market rate. The five lines of estimate #1001 come over as they were. Three use a quantity: 4.5 hr × $62.00 = $279.00, 64 sq ft × $2.25 = $144.00 and 1 lot × $86.40 = $86.40. Two are flat prices: $285.00 and $60.00. Together they make $854.40. Change order #1 came to $335.60, so it goes on as one more line, a flat price. There’s no sales tax in the example.

    So the total is $854.40 + $335.60 = $1,190.00, and the $250.00 deposit already paid leaves a balance due of $1,190.00 − $250.00 = $940.00.

    Questions contractors ask

    What’s the difference between an estimate and an invoice?

    The estimate is the price you offer before the work, for the customer to say yes to. The invoice is the bill: what the customer owes for the work, usually once it’s done. It lists the approved work, change orders included, takes off what’s already paid and shows the balance due.

    How do I put a change order on the invoice?

    Add it as one line, like “Change order #1, approved”, with its total as a flat price, or copy its lines one by one. Put its number under “Change order #s” too, so the customer can match the invoice to what they signed. If a change order took money off, write the lower price on the lines it changed.

    What goes in “payments received”?

    Everything the customer already paid on this job: the deposit and any payment since, added up. It comes off the total, and what’s left is the balance due. Leave it blank if nothing has been paid yet.

    Does it add sales tax?

    Only if you type a rate: the template applies the tax % to the whole subtotal. Whether labor, materials or both are taxable depends on your state and the kind of job. Your state’s rules (or your accountant) decide whether you add any tax line. Leave it blank if you don’t charge sales tax.

    How do I save it as a PDF?

    Press Print or save PDF and choose “Save as PDF” as the printer. On a phone, look under Print or Share. Then send the PDF to your customer by email or text.

    Can I print a blank one to fill in by hand?

    Yes. Leave the lines empty and press Print or save PDF: everything you left empty prints as a blank line to write on. Fill in only your business first and you get your own blank invoice form.

    Are the example terms legal advice?

    No. They’re terms contractors commonly use, not legal advice. If your state has rules for home improvement contracts, follow them; this template doesn’t replace them.

    Where do the numbers go?

    Nowhere. Everything you type stays on this page, in your browser, and nothing is sent to us. It isn’t saved either, so print or save the PDF before you close the page. On talkestimate.com we count that the template was used, never what you typed.
