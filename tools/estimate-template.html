    How the totals add up

    Every amount is one you type. The template only multiplies and adds, in a fixed order, so you can check each line on the printout.

    Line total quantity × unit price (no quantity: the price you typed, a flat price)
    Subtotal every line total, added up
    Sales tax subtotal × tax %, rounded to the cent once
    Total subtotal + sales tax
    Balance due total − deposit

    Each line is rounded to the cent once, and the tax once. A line with no price adds nothing.

    What goes on the estimate, and why

    • Who and where. Your business and how to reach you, the customer and the job address, so there’s no doubt which job this is.
    • A number and two dates. The number lets you both refer to it. “Valid until” keeps last season’s price from coming back when materials go up.
    • One line per task. A customer can see, compare and approve lines. One lump sum is much harder to say yes to.
    • What isn’t included. One line in the notes heads off most arguments later.
    • How to say yes. The deposit, when the rest is due, and a place to sign.

    The example, checked

    The example is a bathroom ceiling repair with five lines (every price is an example, not a market rate). Three lines use a quantity: 4.5 hr × $62.00 = $279.00, 64 sq ft × $2.25 = $144.00 and 1 lot × $86.40 = $86.40. Two are flat prices: $285.00 and $60.00. Together they make an $854.40 subtotal, and a $250.00 deposit leaves a $604.40 balance due.

    Add a 6.5% sales tax and the tax is $55.54 (6.5% of $854.40 is $55.536, rounded once to the cent), so the total is $909.94 and the balance due $659.94.

    Questions contractors ask

    What if a line has no quantity?

    Then it’s a flat price: the price you type is the line total. Use it for a task you price as one amount, like “Replace the bathroom fan.” With a quantity, the line total is quantity × unit price.

    Should I add sales tax?

    Only if your state (and city) has you charge it on this kind of job. Whether labor, materials or both are taxable depends on the state and the type of work, so check your state’s rules. The template applies the tax % to the whole subtotal; leave it blank if you don’t charge sales tax.

    How much deposit can I ask for?

    That’s your call, within your state’s rules: some states limit the deposit on home improvement work before the job starts. The balance due is the total minus the deposit you enter.

    How do I save it as a PDF?

    Press Print or save PDF and choose “Save as PDF” as the printer. On a phone, look under Print or Share. Then send the PDF to your customer by email or text.

    Can I print a blank one to fill in by hand?

    Yes. Leave the lines empty and press Print or save PDF: everything you left empty prints as a blank line to write on. Fill in only your business first and you get your own blank estimate form.

    Are the example terms legal advice?

    No. They’re terms contractors commonly use, not legal advice. If your state has rules for home improvement contracts, follow them; this template doesn’t replace them.

    Where do the numbers go?

    Nowhere. Everything you type stays on this page, in your browser, and nothing is sent to us. It isn’t saved either, so print or save the PDF before you close the page. On talkestimate.com we count that the template was used, never what you typed.
