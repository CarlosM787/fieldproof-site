    Markup vs margin: the same job, two different prices

    Markup is a percentage of your cost. Margin is a percentage of the price. Use the same number for both and you get two different prices — and two different paychecks.

    20% markup
    Materials + labor$1,000.00
    Markup: 20% of $1,000+ $200.00
    Price$1,200.00

    You keep $200 of $1,200 = 16.7% margin

    20% margin
    Materials + labor$1,000.00
    Gross profit: 20% of the price+ $250.00
    Price: $1,000 ÷ (1 − 0.20)$1,250.00

    You keep $250 of $1,250 = 20% margin

    Same costs, same "20%", a $50 difference on a $1,000 job. Repeat that across a year of jobs and it is real money.

    Price from markup price = cost × (1 + markup)
    Price from margin price = cost ÷ (1 − margin)
    Margin of a price margin = (price − cost) ÷ price
    Markup → margin margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup)
    Margin → markup markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin)

    How to use the number you get

    1. Start from what the job costs you. Materials at what you pay, labor at what an hour costs you, and job extras like a dumpster or a sub.
    2. Decide whether overhead goes in the price. If you price on job costs only, your truck, insurance and phone are paid out of the gross profit. The calculator tells you when the price doesn't cover the overhead you entered.
    3. Pick markup or margin on purpose. If you think "I want to keep 25% of every job," that's a margin: use a 33.3% markup, or switch the calculator to margin.
    4. Check what is left. Gross profit still has to pay you and your taxes. If it's thin, the job is thin.

    Questions contractors ask

    Is a 20% markup the same as a 20% margin?

    No. A 20% markup on $1,000 gives $1,200, and you keep 16.7% of that price. A 20% margin needs a price of $1,250. To keep 20% of the price, use a 25% markup.

    Should I mark up materials and labor the same?

    That's your call. Some contractors use one markup on everything; others put a smaller markup on materials and more on labor. You can run each part through this calculator separately and add the prices.

    Does gross margin include overhead?

    No. Gross margin is price minus the job's direct costs (materials, labor, other job costs). Overhead comes out of the gross profit. Enter your overhead and this calculator shows what is left after it, which is still not net profit.

    Why can't I enter a 100% margin?

    Margin is a share of the price. Keeping 100% of the price would mean the job cost you nothing. Any margin below 100% works; the closer you get, the faster the price climbs.

    Where do the numbers go?

    Nowhere. The math runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent to us. On talkestimate.com we count that the calculator was used, never what you typed.
